# Claude and Magic - the eighth big update: the land, the clock and the hearth

A plan for v6.1 to v7.0. The town has a door the party walks out of and a shaft it walks down; this update gives it
a *land* around it and a *clock* over it. The Deep Wood outside the town, with its trees, caves and little dungeons;
mining, with ore of several kinds and the smith's upgrading that wants it; farming grown from the garden's beds into
fields, a mill and a kitchen worth the name; scrolls that bring the party home from below; a day and a night that
turn as the party walks, with the sun and the moon in the HUD and effects that come with them; and a calendar with
seasons and their events. Everything stands on what is there: the garden (`69b-square.js`), the kitchen at home
(`67-home.js`), the smith's three trades and the forge (`63-camp.js`, `68-shops.js`), the gathering nodes and the
alchemist's ingredients (`CM.NODES`, `CM.INGREDIENTS`), the Scroll of Recall (`CM.SCROLLS.recall`), the town as
floor 0 (`23-town.js`), the week's seed (`CM.weeklySeed`) and the clock stub (`CM.clockHour`).

The shape in one line: **a party that farms, mines and forages by day on the land, cooks and forges at home, and
goes down the shaft with a pack it made - and comes home by a scroll when the night or the season says so.**

---

## Two decisions, made here so every phase agrees

- **The clock is the game's, not the wall's.** Time passes by steps (a step a minute; a floor is a couple of
  hours; a camp's rest is the night), so a run is the same every time it is played back and the simulator can
  measure a day. The hour is saved with the run. The real clock (`CM.clockHour`) stops meaning anything but the
  lanterns on the title screen.
- **The calendar is the browser's, as the week's seed already is.** The season and its events come from the real
  date, the way the daily and the weekly seeds do, so two players see the same festival the same week; a setting
  pins a season for whoever wants the harvest fair in July. A run notes the season it began in and keeps it, so a
  save does not change season under the party.

## Phase 0 - The clock, the sun and the moon - v6.1 - DONE

- **Time passes.** `CM.CLOCK = { minute: 1 step, dayLength: 1440 }`; the run keeps `game.clock` (minutes since the
  run began; a new run begins at dawn, 6:00); a camp's rest or the hearth sleeps until the next dawn; a floor's
  stair takes an hour; a fight takes its rounds. `CM.hourOf(game)`, `CM.isNight(game)`, `CM.moonPhase(game)` (a
  29-day moon from the run's day).
- **The HUD** gains a *sky bar* over the view: the sun's and the moon's positions on an arc, the hour in words
  (dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, dusk, evening, night, the small hours), and the moon's phase as its shape. On a
  pad and a touch screen it is just drawn; a tap on it says the hour and the date.
- **What the hour does, on the land and above** (the Deep has no sky: nothing changes below floor 1): the town's
  lanterns and the townsfolk's lines by the hour; the Wood's beasts by night (Phase 2); the farm's crops grow by
  the dawn (Phase 4); the moon's phases on the Wood's caves and the dungeon's undead (a full moon: the undead a
  tenth harder on the first five floors and the Drowned Town's tide higher; a new moon: the Rogue's picks a tenth
  surer and ambushes by night more likely).
- **Saves and films**: the clock is in the save and the replay spec; a film shows the sky bar turn.
- **Tests**: the clock from steps, rests and stairs; dawn after a rest; the moon's period; the HUD's words at each
  hour; a replay keeps the clock.

Size: S-M. Everything after reads this clock.

## Phase 1 - Scrolls of Homecoming - v6.2 - DONE

- **The scroll**: `CM.SCROLLS.homecoming`: read anywhere below (a fight excepted), the party is at its own door;
  the scroll remembers the square it was read on, and read again from the town it takes the party back there -
  the camps' Scroll of Recall's shape, stretched to the top. One in twenty chests from floor 3 carries one; the
  dead drop one in forty (an elite one in ten); the Merchant's House sells one at a price that climbs with the
  deepest floor reached (never the camps: a camp's merchant sells Recall).
- **What the trip costs**: the hour (the climb is the night: the party arrives at dawn, and the clock says so);
  the monsters move on the floor behind (`changeBehind` runs as if a hundred steps passed); a hero who was
  fallen stays fallen until the temple.
- **The home's part**: with a scroll in the pack the home's stash, kitchen and the merchant are a night away from
  anywhere; the Automatic errands know it (a party low on potions below floor 10 with a scroll and gold reads it,
  shops, and reads it again).
- **The bot and the sim**: `--home` counts the trips; the crown rate is held within five points of v6.0 (the
  scroll is a convenience, not a strength).
- **Tests**: a scroll from a chest and from a drop by the seed; up and back to the same square; the night passes;
  the bot's trips.

Size: S.

## Phase 2 - The Deep Wood - v6.3

- **A second surface floor**: `CM.makeWood(seed)`, floor `-1` west of the town through a gate at the west end of
  the main lane (the house moves one square in; the gate is at x = 0). A 31-square map generated by the seed from
  the forest's own rules (not the dungeon's generators): glades joined by paths through trees (a tree is a wall
  the renderer draws as a trunk and a crown: a new wall layer "tree", the floor "grass", the ceiling "sky" by the
  hour), a river with a ford, a hermit's hut, a ruined shrine, a charcoal burner's clearing, and three cave mouths.
- **The Wood by day and by night**: by day, foraging nodes (mushrooms, berries, herbs, deadwood, a beehive: the
  farm's and the kitchen's things - Phases 3 and 4), a woodcutter's stump (deadwood with an axe), deer and boar
  (meat, hides: a new ingredient kind `hide` for the leatherworker in Phase 5) that run from the party; by night,
  wolves and worse that come to it (the dungeon's wandering rules with the Wood's own bands: wolves, a bear, bandits
  with loadouts, and under a full moon a wolf that is more), and the hermit's door shut. The Wood's monsters give
  no renown and little gold; they give hides, meat and the night's danger.
- **The caves** (three mouths; one a day is open, by the day of the run): a short dungeon floor each (`-1.5`,
  `-1.6`, `-1.7`, built as wings are), of the first bands' kinds, with ore veins in the walls (Phase 3), a chest or
  two, and a sub-dungeon's own guardian at the bottom of the third (the Wood's own, not a relic's: a troll under
  the hill with a part that breaks and a hide worth a cloak).
- **The ruined shrine**: a carving in the Wood's own voice (the hill's chapter has a page here: the order's first
  house was in the trees before it was on the hill), read like the dungeon's walls.
- **Getting there and back**: the gate; a step on the Wood's edge says the time; the Wood is never a trap (the
  gate is always open, and a scroll of Homecoming works there too). Automatic walks the Wood only when told to
  (a toggle in its menu: *forage by day*).
- **Tests**: the Wood is the seed's and the same twice; day and night change the bands; a cave is a floor; the
  guardian; the gate from both sides; the renderer's new layers are in the atlas.

Size: L.

## Phase 3 - Mining and the ore - v6.4

- **Five ores**, `CM.ORES`: copper (the Wood's caves and floors 1-10), iron (the caves and floors 6-20), silver
  (floors 11-25; the Dwarven Halls' veins give two), gold (the caves' deepest, floors 21-40), and star-iron (the
  Foundry and the Root's floors, one vein a floor). An *ore vein* is a wall feature with a face; a *pick* (the
  dwarves' pick becomes a tool that is kept: `CM.PICKS`: a copper pick mines copper and iron, an iron pick silver,
  a steel pick everything; a pick wears a point a swing and is reset at the forge) mines it with Space: three
  swings a vein, a chunk a swing, the vein spent for the run. The Artificer mines twice. Mining makes noise: a
  swing calls the floor's nearest band a square closer.
- **The smelter** at the forge (the smith's second level): three chunks make a bar; two bars of a kind and one of
  the kind above make an *alloy bar* (bronze, steel, electrum, star-steel). Bars are the upgrading's coin (Phase 5)
  and sell to the apprentice at an honest price.
- **The vault and the stash** take bars; the weight rules know ore is heavy (a chunk 2, a bar 3: a mining trip is a
  choice about what to carry back).
- **The Wood's charcoal burner** sells charcoal, which the smelter wants one of a bar; deadwood from the Wood makes
  charcoal at his kiln for nothing.
- **Tests**: a vein by the seed, mined in three swings, spent for the run; the pick's wear and the forge's
  resetting; three chunks a bar, the alloys; the noise.

Size: M.

## Phase 4 - The farm - v6.5

- **The garden becomes a farm**, in the Wood's first glade past the gate (the beds in town stay as the herb garden
  for the alchemist): raised with renown like a building (`CM.VILLAGE.farm`, five levels): a field (level 1: four
  rows), an orchard (2), a mill (3), hens (4), a cow and a byre (5). `village.farm` keeps it.
- **Crops by season** (`CM.CROPS`): wheat and barley (spring sown, summer grown, autumn cut), beans and cabbages
  (spring and autumn), turnips (any but winter), hops (summer), apples and pears (the orchard, autumn), herbs
  (the town's beds, any season). A row is sown with seed (bought at the Merchant's House, or kept from a harvest),
  grows a stage every *dawn the party sees* (the clock's day: a run that sleeps at the camps grows the field; a
  run that reads Homecoming at night and sleeps at home grows it too), and is cut at the fourth stage. Weather
  (Phase 7) and the season set the pace. A row left past ripe for three dawns rots.
- **The mill** grinds wheat and barley to flour and malt (a sack a day); the **hens** lay an egg a dawn each (three
  hens); the **cow** gives milk a dawn and wants a cabbage a week; the **byre** keeps the Wood's deer for venison
  twice a season. Crows take a row in ten unless a scarecrow stands (the Wood's deadwood and the party's oldest
  tunic). The farm's things are `kind: "food"` ingredients with a *freshness* (eggs and milk a week; grain and
  flour forever; apples a season).
- **The farmhand**: at level 3 the farm hires one (gold a season) who sows and cuts what you tell him when the
  party is away, so a deep run comes home to a harvest.
- **Automatic** sows and cuts on its errands.
- **Tests**: a row from sowing to cutting by dawns; the season's rule; the mill, the hens, the cow; rot; the
  farmhand across a run.

Size: L.

## Phase 5 - Upgrading and tempering gear - v6.6

- **Upgrading, with a ceiling.** Every weapon, armour, shield and helm has a *grade* (0 to 5, `item.grade`), shown
  as stars on the card and in the name's tail ("Long Sword ★★"). The smith raises a grade for bars of the base's
  metal tier (copper for tiers 0-2, bronze 3-4, iron 5-6, steel 7-8, electrum and star-steel for the rest) and gold
  that climbs; each grade is +1 power and, at 3 and 5, one of the base's implicit words made a tier better. Grade
  is kept through reforging and the vault. Nothing stacks past the sixth update's ceiling: a grade-5 thing is a
  tier-III affix's worth, and a thing may not be both grade 5 and enchanted +3 (the smith says why).
- **Tempering** (the forge's second trade; the smith's third level): a *temper* is one word from the land worked in
  for the run - oak (a tenth less weight), beeswax (water does not wet it), wolf-fat (silent in the dark), bear's
  bile (a bleed on a blow), star-dust (+1 to the element) - from the Wood's and the farm's things; one temper at a
  time, gone at the run's end (a tempered thing says what it is on the card).
- **The leatherworker** (the Wood's hermit, by day): hides into a cloak, boots, a buckler and a quiver (the Ranger
  shoots twice a fight with a full one) - the Wood's own gear line, outside the merchant's.
- **The compendium** gains grades and tempers; the item card shows them; `CM.itemName` writes the stars.
- **Tests**: a grade for bars and gold, the power and the implicit; the ceiling; a temper for the run and gone
  after; a cloak from hides; a replay keeps the grades.

Size: M.

## Phase 6 - Cooking as a trade - v6.7

- **The kitchen** (home level 3) becomes a trade: `CM.DISHES` (thirty): bread (flour), porridge (barley), an
  omelette (eggs), a stew (meat and turnip), a pie (meat, flour, an egg), apple cake, a roast (venison, apples),
  honey cakes (flour, honey), a posset (milk, egg, honey), beer (malt and hops, a week in the cellar), cheese
  (milk, a week), the hunter's breakfast (eggs, meat, bread), the miner's pasty (meat, turnip, flour: a meal that
  keeps three floors), the fisherman's soup (the Wood's river: fish, a new node), the dwarves' bread remade, the
  queen's cake (a festival's), and the dungeon's own: rat on a stick, kobold stew, spore-cap fry, troll's heart
  (the Phase 7 guardian's part).
- **What a dish does**: every dish feeds (hunger to nothing) and one thing besides, for a floor count by the dish:
  +1 to a stat, +hit, +defence, mending a round, resist an element, the torch slower, the party's morale, a second
  wind at a quarter health; the better dishes two things. A dish *travels*: it is a thing in the pack with a
  freshness; eaten at a camp or on the floor. One dish's effect at a time (eating another replaces it).
- **The cook**: a hero may take the kitchen's apron (`h.cook` level 0-5, raised a level every five dishes made):
  a cook's dishes last a floor longer a level, and at level 3 the cook may make a dish from the field kit on the
  floor (a torch's fire).
- **A hero's favourite** (by origin and class, from `CM.ORIGINS`): the favourite dish gives +2 morale and a line.
- **The merchant's bench and the tavern** sell the simple dishes at a price; the tavern's round becomes a meal.
- **Tests**: a dish from its things, its effect for its floors, freshness, one at a time; the cook's levels; the
  favourite; the bot eats what it carries.

Size: M.

## Phase 7 - The calendar, the seasons and their events - v6.8

- **Four seasons** by the real date (and a setting to pin one): spring (the Wood's herbs and the farm's sowing; rain
  two days in five), summer (long days: dawn at five, night at nine; the Wood's berries; the beehive full), autumn
  (the harvest; the orchard; mist on the Drowned Town's floors: the tide a square higher), winter (short days;
  snow in the Wood: tracks show the beasts, the river frozen and fordable anywhere; the town's well frozen; the
  farm asleep; the dungeon's first band colder: frost does a tenth more above floor 5). The season is written into
  the run at its start.
- **Weather** by the day of the run and the season: clear, rain, mist, wind, snow; it changes the Wood (rain fills
  the river, mist hides the night's beasts and the party both, wind takes the smell of the party from the beasts)
  and the farm's pace.
- **Twelve festivals**, one a month, keyed to the calendar the way the weekly seed is (`CM.FESTIVALS`): the Sowing
  (seeds half price), Midsummer (the tavern's round free and the townsfolk dancing in the square at night), the
  Harvest Fair (market day all week, the farm's yield doubled), the Night of Bells (the bell-founder's boy hears
  one; the Queen's Ring's lull lasts a turn longer), the Feast of the Dead (the memorial's names read at the well,
  a candle free, the Ossuary's priests gentle), Midwinter (the Lich King's court sits a floor higher; the Drowned
  Queen sings on the first floor's water; the home's hearth gives two rests), the Sexton's Day, the Dwarves'
  Letter, and four of the hill's own. Each has a line from the lore bible (`LORE.md` gains a calendar table first).
- **The dungeon's red-letter days**: the daily seed's mutators take the season's colour (winter dailies carry
  Gloom more often; summer dailies Swift).
- **The HUD** shows the date under the sky bar; the notice board in the square says the festival; the chronicler's
  house keeps a year's calendar with the runs marked on it.
- **Tests**: the season from a date; a pinned season; each festival's rule in a fixed situation; the weather by the
  day; a run keeps its season through a save.

Size: M-L.

## Phase 8 - Around the land - v7.0

- **The balance pass**: fifty seeds on Fair with `--home`, `--wood` and `--farm` (the bot forages a day, mines a
  cave, cooks and upgrades once); the crown rate within five points of v6.0 with all of it, and the floor-median
  unchanged; the sinks of the sixth update re-measured with the bars and the dishes in the economy.
- **Automatic** gains a *day in the land* routine (forage, mine the open cave, sow or cut, cook, forge, then the
  shaft) and a *come home by night* rule (a Homecoming scroll read when the clock says the small hours and the
  pack is heavy or the party is worn).
- **The gamepad and touch**: the sky bar's tap, the farm's rows and the field's picker, the kitchen's lists.
- **Films**: the sky bar turns in a film; a film of a day in the Wood plays.
- **Words as data**: the land's words (the Wood's places, the ores, the crops, the dishes, the festivals) through
  `12-text.js`; `tools/words.js` counts them.
- **The lore bible** (`LORE.md`) gains the calendar's table and the Wood's page; the README's upgrade sections;
  the plan's status; the version.

Size: S-M.

## Risks

- **A second generator.** The Wood is new ground: its own generator, renderer layers and bands. It is kept out of
  the dungeon's generators and fixtures entirely (floor -1 and its caves are made by `makeWood`, as the town is by
  `makeTown`), and the dungeon's floors 1 to 50 do not change by a cell.
- **Time as a mechanic can be a chore.** Nothing *waits* on the clock except the farm's dawns, and those happen
  while the party does what it was doing anyway (rests, camps, floors). No timer runs while the player reads a
  screen; a step is a minute and a dialog is no steps.
- **Two clocks.** The game's clock (steps) and the browser's calendar (seasons, festivals) are kept apart on
  purpose: the first is replayable, the second shared. A run keeps the season it began in, so a save is stable.
- **Power creep, again.** Grades, tempers and dishes all add. The ceiling of the sixth update holds: a grade-5
  thing is worth a tier-III word, grades and the +3 enchant do not both go to the top, a temper is one word for
  one run, a dish is one effect at a time. The balance pass (Phase 8) is the gate; the crown rate on Fair with
  every system in play stays within five points of v6.0.
- **The Wood's danger by night** must not kill parties that just wanted to forage: the gate is always a few
  squares away, the first glade is safe at any hour, and the Wood's beasts give renown nothing - there is no
  reason to be there at night but choice.
- **Saves.** The clock, the season, the farm, the ores and the grades are new fields; the farm lives in the village
  store (schema 5, migrated and tested against a v6.0 export). A save from before the Wood simply has no floor -1
  yet; it is made when first walked.
- **The bot.** Each phase gives it the thing (read the scroll, walk the Wood by day, mine, sow, cook, upgrade) or
  the sim measures nothing; Phase 8 ties them into one day.

## Not in this plan, and why

- **A world map with other towns.** One town, one wood, one shaft: the land is a day's walk across, by design.
- **Fishing as a minigame.** The river gives fish as a node gives moss. No reels.
- **Animal taming, mounts, pets beyond the familiars.** The cow is a cow.
- **A trading economy between seasons (buy low, sell high).** Prices move only by the festivals' rules; gold is
  not a market.
- **Real-time weather or a clock that runs while the game is closed.** The farm grows by the dawns the party sees,
  not the ones it misses; nothing rots in your absence but what the plan says (a row left ripe three dawns).
- **Building your own house's rooms or placing furniture.** The home is raised by levels; a floor plan is not a
  decision this game asks.
