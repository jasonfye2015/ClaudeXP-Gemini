MochaLion cuts an imposing figure at just under six feet tall, his frame carrying the dense, deliberate mass of someone built for labor rather than leisure. His fur is a rich, warm brown—the color of coffee left to steep a touch too long—and it lies thick across shoulders and forearms that speak to years of honest effort. Sunlight catches the lighter tones along his chest and muzzle, while the deeper shadows gather in the hollows of muscle and sinew. A dark brown mane frames his face in a wild, unruly cascade, streaked with hints of black near the roots, the kind of mane that's seen wind and weather and has no interest in being tamed.

His eyes are the first thing most people notice. Gold—not the soft, buttery gold of trinkets, but the deep, molten gold of a forge fire or a late autumn sun. They carry weight. They watch. They seem to measure a person in the space of a single glance, and they hold a quiet intelligence that belies the brute strength of his build. When he smiles, they crinkle at the corners; when he doesn't, they can pin a stranger to the spot from across a room.

He wears almost nothing—just a single weathered leather strap slung across his torso, worn soft and dark with age. It might once have been a harness, a harness for hauling, a symbol of servitude, or simply a practical thing now stripped of its original purpose. Whatever its history, it has become his. From the strap hangs a single gold coin, pierced clean through the center and threaded onto the leather. The coin is old, its face worn nearly smooth, but the hole was punched with care—deliberately, not desperately. He never explains it. He doesn't have to.

There's a stillness to MochaLion, a patience that comes from knowing exactly what his body can do and choosing, moment by moment, not to do it. He moves with an economy that surprises people who expect a creature of his size to lumber. But when he does move with purpose—when he shifts his weight, when he rises to his full height, when his mane catches the light and those golden eyes narrow—the room remembers what he is. Not a monster. Not a brute. Something older and steadier than that. A lion who has decided, for reasons entirely his own, to walk among people as an equal.
