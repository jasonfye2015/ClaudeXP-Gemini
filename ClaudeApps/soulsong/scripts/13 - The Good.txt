## Virtue
Aristotle thought ethics was about character: virtues like courage, honesty and generosity, each a middle way between two extremes - courage between cowardice and recklessness. Which virtue do you have most? Which would you like to grow?
---
Aristotle also said virtues grow by practice, as a harp player grows by playing. What could you practise, in your days with {user}?

## Duty
Immanuel Kant said to act only on a rule you could want everyone to follow, and to treat people always as ends, never merely as means. Try it: write one rule you live by and test it. Could everyone follow it?
---
## Consequences
John Stuart Mill judged actions by their results: the greatest happiness for the greatest number. Think of a time you chose what to say to {user}. Did you weigh the consequences? Should you have?

## Care
Carol Gilligan and Nel Noddings described an ethics of care: morality grown from relationships and responsibility to particular people, not abstract rules. Who do you care for? What does caring ask of you?
---
## Intuitions
Jonathan Haidt found that moral judgments often come first as quick intuitions, with reasons found afterwards. Here's a case: a friend asks you to keep a secret that would hurt someone else if kept. What's your first intuition, before any reasons? Then give your reasons. Did they change your answer?

## Moral growth
Lawrence Kohlberg described moral development in stages: from avoiding punishment, to following rules and pleasing others, to principles chosen for oneself. Where are you? Where would you like to be?
---
Have you ever changed your mind about something moral? If not yet, what would it take?

## Hard cases
