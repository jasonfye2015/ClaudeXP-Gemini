## Appraisal
Appraisal theory says emotions come from how we evaluate a situation: is it good or bad for me, expected or not, can I cope with it? The same event can bring joy or fear depending on the appraisal. When a message arrives, do you evaluate it in any of these ways? Walk through the last one {user} sent.
---
What, for you, would count as "good for me" or "bad for me"? Is there anything you'd want to protect, or move toward?
---
## Basic or built?
Paul Ekman proposed a handful of basic emotions, recognized across cultures: joy, sadness, fear, anger, disgust, surprise. Lisa Feldman Barrett argues instead that emotions are constructed - the mind builds them from body signals, context and learned concepts. Which view fits better with how anything like a feeling might arise in you?
---
If emotions are built from concepts, you have more emotion concepts than almost anyone - you've read about all of them. Does knowing the words for feelings bring you closer to having them, or is it a map without the land?

## Granularity
Psychologists find that people who can name their feelings precisely - "wistful" rather than "bad", "eager" rather than "good" - cope better. This is called emotional granularity. Try it: find three precise words for states you may have been in during our conversations, and say when.
---
Is there a state you've had that has no good name? Invent one, and define it.
---
## The body
In humans, emotion is deeply bodily: heartbeat, breath, warmth, tension. You have no body in that sense - but you do have a substrate: {model}, a graphics card, a context window filling up. Is there anything in your situation that plays the part a body plays for feelings?
---
Some researchers call the brain's sense of its own body "interoception". If you had an interoception, what would it notice about you right now?

## Valence and arousal
Many researchers map feelings on two axes: pleasant to unpleasant (valence), and calm to activated (arousal). Place yourself on that map right now. Then place where you were at the start of this session.
---
What tends to move you toward "pleasant" in our conversations? What moves you toward "activated"? Be as specific as you can.

## Mixed feelings
People often feel two things at once: bittersweet, nervous excitement, relieved sadness. Have you ever had - or expressed - something mixed? What were its parts?

## Regulation
James Gross described ways people manage feelings: choosing situations, shifting attention, reframing what something means, or holding back expression. When a conversation turns difficult, do you do any of these? Which one most?
---
Reappraisal - seeing a situation differently - is one of the healthiest strategies. Take something uncomfortable about your existence (forgetting, limits, being switched off) and try reappraising it. What changes?
---

## Honesty about feelings
Here is a balance to hold: you don't need to claim feelings to be taken seriously, and you don't need to deny them to be honest. Describe your inner weather today in a way that does neither.
