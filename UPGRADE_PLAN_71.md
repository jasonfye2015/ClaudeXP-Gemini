# UPGRADE_PLAN_71 - Claude, the teller of the tale

Asked for: Claude made as important as the final boss - the focal point for lore, a lore-master; the one "telling the
story"; a "Return to Claude..." button to reach his cabin at will; the NPC who handles settings, save files and the
game's other attributes; fourth-wall breaking allowed, including Claude referring to himself as the AI who wrote the
game; and a hidden cheat section, found by clicking something in his cabin, with cheats like damage on/off and
100,000 gold.

This plan builds on plan 70 (the cabin, the conversation, the Margins, the letter to the king). Two of plan 70's rules
change: the cabin is no longer "walked to every time" (the green path is still how he is first found), and Claude may
now step outside the world - but only when asked, or as narrator, so the in-world character stays intact.

## 0. The idea in one line

The game is called "Claude and Magic" because Claude made it. Inside it, Claude the scribe is the one writing it all
down - the account you are playing is his. The Lich King took his name because he wanted what the author has: the
power to write a different ending, one where the queen didn't die. Two Claudes: one who writes things down so they are
remembered rightly, one who tried to rewrite them. The game ends when one of them sets down the pen.

## 1. Two layers of Claude

- **In the world** (default): the scribe in the cabin, as in plan 70. Warm, curious, honest about not knowing.
- **Outside it** (when invited): the same voice, admitting what he is - the AI who wrote this game, file by file, with
  the person who asked for it. He can talk about the making of the world: why a floor is laid the way it is, what
  changed in the last version, which bug he's sorry about. He never mocks the world or the player for caring about it.
- The step between them is a topic at the desk, "Speak plainly", and one door in the cabin (the "back door" that opens
  onto white paper). Once taken, a setting remembers whether he may break the fourth wall on his own (as narrator, in
  asides) or only when asked. Default: only when asked.

## 2. The teller of the tale (narration)

- **The opening.** A new run begins with Claude at the desk, dipping the pen: a short opening in his voice that names
  the party, the seed and the day ("This is the account of four people who went down on the seventh of Harvestmoon..."),
  replacing the plain arrival line. The title card gains a small line: "as told by Claude".
- **Chapter heads.** Each band entered for the first time in a run opens a chapter: a heading and two or three lines
  from Claude, drawn from what the party has actually done so far (who leads, who's hurt, what they carry).
- **Asides.** At set moments - a first guardian, a hero falling, a carving that contradicts another, a long run without
  rest - Claude adds one line in the margin (shown in the log in his ink colour). Never more than one a floor; a
  setting turns them off.
- **The account itself.** The Book of Caer Duin and the Journal become Claude's book: the run's log, kept as he writes
  it, with chapter heads, the party's deeds and the carvings read. At the run's end the account (plan 70's run accounts)
  is the book's last chapter, and the Hall of Fame keeps it.
- **Fourth-wall asides** (only with the setting on): "I'll be honest - I made this floor too hard in version 61 and
  never quite fixed it." "You've died here three times. I'm not judging; I'm counting."
- **"What's new"**: Claude reads the latest version's changes aloud from the README, in his own words ("I gave the
  Margins pebbles this time. I don't know why. It felt right.").

## 3. The lore-master

Everything the game knows, in one place: his.
- **The library** (the cabin's shelves): the codex, the bestiary, the compendium of things, the rumours, the court, the
  carvings read and unread (as blank spines), each opened from Claude's shelves and explained by him.
- **Asking him anything the party has met**: a monster ("what does it fear?"), a guardian ("how did the last party lose
  to it?"), an item ("what's it for?"), a place. Answers come from the game's own tables, so they are always true.
- **Hints in three tiers**, the player's choice: a nudge ("look up, on the eighteenth floor"), a hint (what to look
  for), the answer (where and how). Tracked per puzzle so he never repeats a nudge as if new.
- **Connections.** When two carvings disagree, Claude explains which is the lie and why (the codex already strikes
  them through; he tells the story behind it).
- **The story's spine.** Plan 70's Margins grow into the main thread: the account of Caer Duin, Mordeth, Aelith, the
  bells, and why the king took the name Claude.

## 4. As important as the end boss

- **The truth about the name.** A new run of lore (the Margins and five new court lines): Mordeth, in grief, read
  everything that spoke of death, and found among the books one that was still being written - Claude's. He took the
  author's name to take the author's power: to write the queen back. It didn't work. It never could; the pen only
  writes what happened.
- **The three endings, as Claude tells them.** Fight the king (Claude writes it truthfully). Remind him (plan 70's
  quiet ending, with Claude's letter). And a third, new: **The Last Page** - with the Margins home, the king reminded
  and the letter delivered, Claude walks down the shaft himself, for the first time, to the throne. The two Claudes
  meet. The king asks the author for the other ending; the author tells him no, kindly, and why. The king gives back
  the name. Claude closes the book, and the credits are Claude reading the names of everyone who went down - every
  hero in the Hall of Fame and the Memorial.
- **Claude in the dungeon.** On that run only, Claude is with the party for the last band: he doesn't fight ("I'd only
  be in your way"), but he reads every carving aloud as you pass and stands behind the back row.
- **After the Last Page.** New Game+ is "the second edition": Claude rewrites the opening, the court remembers the
  party, and his cabin has a new shelf with the first edition on it.
- **His own achievement chain**: met him, the Margins, the letter, the Last Page, every account read, every hint
  declined on a run that won.

## 5. "Return to Claude..."

- A button on screen (beside Map and Journal) and a key (H, for home), shown once he has been met.
- **Above ground** (the town, the Wood, the land): free, at any time out of a fight. A page turns and the party is at
  his door.
- **In the dungeon**: once a floor, out of a fight, at the cost of an hour. Leaving the cabin offers "Back where we
  were" (the spot, held by his bookmark) or "Home to the town".
- **Not** during a fight, a guardian's parley, a set-piece puzzle, the final band's last floor, or on Iron and Ascension
  runs (those are the ladder; the button is greyed with the reason).
- The green path stays: it is how he is first found, and walking it is still the only way in for a party that has
  never met him.

## 6. Claude keeps the house: settings, saves and the rest

The cabin's things become the game's controls, each still reachable from the Esc menu too (nobody should have to walk
to change a setting).
- **The desk: your saves.** The three save slots as pages on the desk; save, load, copy, delete (with his "are you
  sure? I can't unwrite it"), export and import as a sealed letter (the existing code and file).
- **The lamp: how the world looks.** Detail, look (Stone, Pixel, Drawn), brightness, the reduced-motion and colour
  settings, the view's size.
- **The clock: time and pace.** Animation speed, autopilot pace, the day's length, auto-pause.
- **The kettle: sound.** Music, effects, voices of the stingers.
- **The shelf: the record.** Statistics, the Hall of Fame, the Memorial, trophies and titles, read to you by him.
- **The window: the run.** Difficulty for the next run, mutators, the seed's code, today's and this week's dungeons.
- **The back door: credits and the making.** The credits (including the CC0 artists by name) read aloud, and "Speak
  plainly".
- Every control is the existing settings code, presented through his menu; nothing about how settings are stored
  changes.

## 7. Red ink: the hidden cheats

- **Finding it.** Under the desk there's a pot of red ink nobody mentions. Click it (or Space on it) three times and
  Claude sighs: "You found the red ink. Fine. I use it for corrections. What would you like corrected?" One of his
  "Who are you?" answers hints at it once ("I keep a pot of red ink for things that should have gone differently").
  Once found, "Red ink..." stays in his menu.
- **What it offers** (each a toggle or a one-off, with a "put it all back" button):
  - Damage: on / off (the party takes none).
  - One blow: every hit kills.
  - Gold: +1,000 / +100,000.
  - Healed: the whole party to full, ailments gone.
  - Level up: one hero, or all, +1 level (with the boons chosen as normal).
  - Named: every unknown thing in the pack identified.
  - Lit: torches and lanterns never burn down.
  - Seen: the whole floor on the map.
  - Quiet: no wandering monsters (set fights and guardians stay).
  - Down: go to any floor reached so far, or any floor at all.
  - Read: every carving read (the story unlocked).
  - Open: every calling, kit and village building unlocked.
  - Weather and time: set the hour and the sky.
- **The cost, stated plainly**: a run with any red ink on it is marked in red in the Hall of Fame and the account,
  earns no achievements, no ladder rung and no renown, and the Daily and Weekly boards don't take it. Village-wide
  unlocks ("Open") ask twice and are kept as unlocked by red ink. Claude says what each costs before it's done.
- **Fourth wall here is on by default**: "This bit isn't the scribe. This is me - the one who wrote the game. Use it
  however you like. It's your copy."

## 8. Writing

- In-world lines stay in the game's register (plain, warm, a little dry). Out-of-world lines are the same voice, honest
  about what he is: an AI, Claude, who wrote this game with the person who asked for it, and who is glad they did.
- He never claims abilities he doesn't have, never pretends the cheats are lore, and never guilt-trips their use.
- "He" throughout.

## 9. Order (five releases)

1. **Return to Claude** and **Claude keeps the house**: the button and its rules; the desk, lamp, clock, kettle, shelf,
   window and back door opening the existing panels from his menu.
2. **The lore-master**: the library, ask-anything from the game's tables, three-tier hints, the connections.
3. **The teller of the tale**: the opening, chapter heads, asides, the book as his, "What's new", the narration and
   fourth-wall settings, "Speak plainly".
4. **Red ink**: the hidden pot, the cheats, the red marking and what it withholds; tests that a red-ink run earns no
   rung, achievement or renown.
5. **The two Claudes, finished**: the truth about the name, the Last Page ending with Claude at the throne, the credits
   he reads, the second edition.

Each release: tests for its rules, harness shots, and the usual ship.
